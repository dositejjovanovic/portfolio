I am deeply honored to have had the opportunity last night to accept the Youth Recognition “Remembering Hilda Dajč” on behalf of the Union of Secondary School Students of Serbia.

The award was presented for the “Crisis School Traffic Light” project, an initiative through which we monitored the state of students’ rights and freedoms throughout 2025, including participation in decision-making, freedom of expression, and the overall level of democracy within school communities. It means a lot to see the importance of secondary school students’ voices recognized when speaking about challenges that directly affect education and young people’s everyday lives.

At the same time, this recognition does not belong to just one project or one individual. It belongs to the thousands of secondary school students who, through 23 years of work within the Union of Secondary School Students of Serbia, have shown that young people can be responsible, engaged, and committed to creating positive social change. It belongs to every local team, activist, volunteer, and member who continuously invests their time, energy, and knowledge into making schools more open, democratic, and supportive environments for students.

I am especially grateful that, alongside the jury award, the Union of Secondary School Students of Serbia also received the “Voice of Youth” recognition, decided by Belgrade high school students themselves. Support and trust coming directly from peers is, without doubt, one of the most meaningful forms of recognition we could receive.

The ceremony was held at University Library “Svetozar Marković” in the presence of representatives of civil society organizations, educational institutions, and youth organizations, as well as Edward Ferguson.
Thank you to the organizers and the jury for this recognition. For all of us in the Union, this award is a strong motivation to continue building a society in which young people’s voices are not only heard, but truly valued.
